GREEN / publication-ready with source citation:
- Government Springs predates Enid as a Chisholm Trail-era watering/camping location.
- 1900 waterworks bond approval and contemporary engineering report of a 26-foot-diameter well.
- Later USGS-documented 20-foot-diameter, 47-foot-deep brick municipal well.
- 1911/1918 main plant gallery-well system.
- Bowles v. City of Enid (1952) and City of Enid v. Crow (1957) groundwater litigation.
- Canton federal storage history, OKC temporary federal use beginning 1955, 1990 agreement existence/date, WRDA 1990 reassignment framework.
- 2014 alternatives study: groundwater viable and cheaper; Kaw selected for strategic advantages including source diversification.
- Kaw initial 10.5 MGD, expandable program to 21 MGD.
- Current project supplements groundwater; 35% aquifer-demand reduction is a stated goal, not yet a measured result.
- WIFIA $53M was offered/invited but not ultimately used.

YELLOW / use qualified wording:
- 26-foot well: publish as what the 1900 engineering journal reported; precise location/photo not established.
- Modern Water Works pond: strong historical continuity, but exact identity as 1921 “frog pond” not conclusively proven.
- Approx. $330M initial Kaw phase: current reported approximate figure; final audited closeout pending.
- Phase II: program designed for 21 MGD; exact equipment-by-equipment expansion list and trigger not publicly established.

- The low structure in the Early Water Works photo is the 26-foot well.
- Today's pond definitely is the 1921 frog pond.
- Enid “owned half of Canton Lake.”
- Enid received $750,000 in the 1990 Canton deal (unverified).
- Oklahoma City paid Enid rent throughout 1955–1990 (unverified).
- Fracking materially caused Enid groundwater declines (not established).
- $475M is the final cost of the 10.5-MGD phase opening Aug. 21.
- Enid received a $53M WIFIA loan.
