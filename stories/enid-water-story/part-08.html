August 2026

Enid’s newest water source begins roughly 70 miles from home. Most of the system is invisible: an intake, buried pipeline, pumping and controls working before the water reaches the city.

Why Kaw finally happened

Kaw was not the cheapest alternative studied. Canton was closer. Groundwater remained viable.

But Kaw offered a second kind of source in a different watershed while allowing Enid to keep its groundwater system.

The city was no longer simply drilling farther from home. It was changing the architecture of its supply.

A new kind of treatment

The modern surface-water plant begins at approximately 10.5 MGD of treatment capacity and was planned for future expansion toward 21 MGD.

Exactly when Enid would need that expansion, and the complete equipment required, remain future questions.

And the wells?

They stay.

Kaw supplements rather than erases the groundwater system. That means the infrastructure Enid spent generations building remains part of the city’s water portfolio.

The City’s stated goal is to reduce average-day demand on the regional aquifers by roughly 35 percent. That is a project goal, not yet a measured result.

FOLLOW THE MONEY

Different Kaw figures answer different questions. The initial phase has been publicly described around $330 million; larger figures around $475 million have been associated with broader/full build-out concepts. Audited debt principal is a different number again.

The final audited project closeout cost was not yet available when this chapter was prepared, so this series does not manufacture one.

Back where we started

Before there was an Enid, people stopped in this area because there was water.

Then came a city, and almost immediately the city needed more.

Over 133 years, Enid dug bigger, reached farther and eventually crossed northern Oklahoma for another source.

The technology changed. The scale changed. The geography changed.

The question remained remarkably familiar:

Where will Enid’s next gallon of water come from?

In 2026, Kaw becomes part of the answer.

Not the entire answer. Part of it.

One year from now

Enid Public Record intends to preserve today’s projections and return in August 2027 with a simple question: Did it perform as promised?

By then, actual records can be compared with the expectations documented today — Kaw production, groundwater pumping, operating costs, lake conditions, demand and reliability.
