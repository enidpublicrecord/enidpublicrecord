MASTER TIMELINE

A water system that kept moving outward

Before 1893

Government Springs

The springs were already a watering and camping place associated with the Chisholm Trail era before Enid existed.

1894

Waterworks already on the public agenda

Within Enid's first year, local newspaper discussion was already treating municipal waterworks as a needed improvement.

1900

Voters approve $40,000 for waterworks

A contemporary engineering journal reported that the system would draw from a well described as 26 feet in diameter.

1911–1918

Main Water Works and underground galleries

Federal records later documented a deep central pump pit, underground well galleries and expansion of the gallery system.

1940s–1980s

Enid goes farther for groundwater

Carrier, Van Buren, Northwest, Drummond, Ames, Cleo Springs and Ringwood became pieces of an increasingly regional supply system.

1948–1990

Canton: storage reserved, used by OKC, then reassigned

Enid's federally authorized Canton storage was never developed into a delivery system. Oklahoma City used storage under federal contracts beginning in 1955; Enid and OKC reached an agreement in 1990, followed by congressional reassignment.

2009–2014

Long-term groundwater risk is formally studied

Engineering studies evaluated additional groundwater, new reservoirs and Kaw. Groundwater remained viable and cheaper on paper; Kaw offered source diversification.

2016

Voters fund Kaw

Enid voters approved a dedicated sales-tax financing structure for Kaw and associated water projects.

2026

Kaw treatment facility opens

The initial 10.5-MGD phase is designed to supplement, not replace, Enid's groundwater system and can be expanded to 21 MGD.

OPEN QUESTIONS

Questions we're still working to answer

Some pieces of Enid's water history remain unresolved. Rather than fill those gaps with assumptions, Enid Public Record will continue looking for the underlying records and update this story when stronger evidence becomes available.

  • Exactly what consideration Enid received under the April 17, 1990 Canton agreement.
  • Whether today's Water Works pond is the exact 1921 “frog pond” or a later/reworked descendant.
  • The precise location and independent Enid corroboration of the 1900 well reported as 26 feet in diameter.
  • When Enid would need to expand the Kaw system from its initial 10.5-MGD capacity toward the planned 21-MGD build-out, and exactly what additional equipment that expansion would require.
  • The complete OWRB administrative record for Enid’s modern Kaw water right, including priority, conditions and acquisition history.
