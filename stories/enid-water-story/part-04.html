AUGUST 17

Enid Goes Looking for Water

As Enid grew, its water system expanded outward. By the mid-20th century, securing municipal groundwater meant pipelines, leases, condemnation and litigation across northwest Oklahoma.

  • Verified: Bowles v. City of Enid documented a 19-mile water main and competing claims involving Ames and Okeene.
  • Balance: City of Enid v. Crow documented that municipal pumping could also harm neighboring users and create liability.
  • Expansion: Drummond, Ames, Cleo Springs and Ringwood became major pieces of the regional well system.
  • Shared resource: irrigation and municipal supply both draw from regional aquifers; we do not assign unsupported percentages of decline to agriculture.
Publication note: This series is built from a verified fact ledger. Claims that remain unresolved are labeled instead of guessed.
