AUGUST 17

Enid Goes Looking for Water

As Enid grew, its water system expanded outward. By the mid-20th century, securing municipal groundwater meant pipelines, leases, condemnation and litigation across northwest Oklahoma.

  • Bowles v. City of Enid documented a 19-mile water main and competing groundwater claims involving Ames and Okeene.
  • City of Enid v. Crow showed the other side of the issue: municipal pumping could harm neighboring users and create liability.
  • Drummond, Ames, Cleo Springs and Ringwood became major pieces of an increasingly regional municipal well system.
  • Irrigation and municipal supply both draw from regional aquifers. The available record does not support assigning a specific share of groundwater decline to agriculture.
