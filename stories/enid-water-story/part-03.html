What's Under the Water Works?

A pond, giant wells, underground galleries and later clearwells turn one ordinary-looking Enid property into more than a century of water history.

There is a pond on West Chestnut. Most people driving past it probably do not give it much thought. We did not either — until we started looking backward.

The Water Works property is a place where Enid’s history accumulated in layers: giant wells, underground galleries, reservoirs, later clearwells and infrastructure built beside what came before.

Federal geologists were studying Enid more than a century ago

Enid’s groundwater attracted federal attention early. A 1915 U.S. Geological Survey study examined groundwater for irrigation in the Enid area. A decade later, B. C. Renick’s 1925 report, Additional Ground-Water Supplies for the City of Enid, Oklahoma, focused directly on the city’s search for more municipal water.

That 1925 report is one of the most valuable surviving technical snapshots of early Enid water infrastructure and the geology beneath it.

The “frog pond”

In 1921, an Enid newspaper described an old open reserve reservoir using a wonderfully unscientific name: the “frog pond.”

The old reservoir could still provide emergency water when a newer covered reservoir was giving trouble.

Thirty years later, a municipal reservoir was still being discussed on West Chestnut. Today, a pond remains at the Water Works.

WHAT WE KNOW — AND WHAT WE DO NOT
  • 1921: an old open municipal reserve reservoir was called the “frog pond.”
  • 1951: a municipal reservoir was reported on West Chestnut.
  • Today: a pond remains at the Water Works.

Still unresolved: whether today’s pond is physically the same reservoir, or a later enlarged or reworked descendant.

Then came the circles

Later modernization brought the enormous circular clearwells visible at the Water Works. They belong to a newer generation of the site, not the earliest reservoir system.

The chronology matters because the property can otherwise tempt us into identifying visible structures as things they are not.
