ENID'S WATER STORY • PART 5 OF 8

The Road Not Taken

Enid's Canton story began before the lake was finished. Congress authorized Canton for flood control in 1938, construction began in December 1940 and World War II temporarily halted the work. The Corps reports that dam construction was substantially completed in May 1948; the conservation pool finished filling in July, and Corps histories describe the overall project as completed by late 1948. Canton was formally dedicated in May 1949.

Federal acts in 1946 and 1948 added irrigation and municipal water-supply storage, including municipal storage for Enid authorized on June 30, 1948. But Enid did not develop access to that storage. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers says Oklahoma City began using Canton storage through a series of five-year federal contracts in 1955. Congress later authorized reassignment of the irrigation and water-supply storage to Oklahoma City through the Water Resources Development Act of 1990. The Corps history reports that the project cost $11 million to build.

Canton was closer. So why didn’t Enid bring the water home?
